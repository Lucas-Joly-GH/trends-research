Every contract the book can trade, with the specification needed to check any number on this site. This is the file the pipeline itself reads — instrument_mapping.csv — not a description of it.

How to use it. A position’s move for a session is quantity × (close − open) × point value × FX. The quantity and both prices are on the Journal page, the point value is here, and the FX rate is the contract’s currency against the dollar on that date. Commission on one side of a trade is half the round-trip cost, converted at the same rate.

Contracts

Point value is what one full point of price is worth in the contract’s own currency; tick size is the smallest increment the exchange quotes. Round-trip cost is charged half per side, which is why a roll costs a full round trip rather than nothing.

How the round-trip cost is built, and what it is not. It is an assumption, not a fee schedule, and it has two terms: one full tick + a fee floor of about $5 equivalent. The tick stands for crossing the spread once each way; the fee floor covers commission, exchange and clearing. Against published rates a competitive broker on a US venue pays roughly $4.10–4.70 round trip, so the floor sits deliberately above it, in every currency.
